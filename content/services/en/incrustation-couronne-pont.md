intro: >-
  A crown covers a fragile tooth to protect it. A bridge replaces a missing
  tooth by resting on the neighbouring ones. At Studio De Facto, these pieces
  are designed digitally and made in our workshop, in zirconia, ceramic or resin
  depending on the case.
variantes:
  - nom: Inlay
    desc: Repairs part of the tooth.
  - nom: Crown
    desc: Covers the whole tooth.
  - nom: Bridge
    desc: Replaces a missing tooth.
whenToConsult:
  - A tooth is cracked or has lost a large part of its structure.
  - A tooth has had a root canal (especially at the back of the mouth).
  - 'An old crown has come loose, broken or no longer fits.'
  - A tooth is missing and you want a fixed solution.
bonASavoir: >-
  A crown needs the same care as a natural tooth. The gum and the root
  underneath can still decay.
treatmentSteps:
  - >-
    A complete examination beforehand, and stage 1 (biological stabilisation)
    completed.
  - >-
    Assessment of the abutment and of the periodontium: residual decay, cracks,
    vitality of the tooth, health of the gum and bone. A root canal or a
    build-up of the abutment may be needed.
  - >-
    Preparation of the tooth under local anaesthetic, then a digital impression
    with the intra-oral scanner, or a conventional impression depending on
    complexity. Shade taking and photographs.
  - >-
    A printed temporary restoration, to protect the tooth and validate the shape
    and the bite.
  - >-
    Computer design and fabrication in the workshop: zirconia, pressed ceramic
    or hand-layered porcelain.
  - >-
    Try-in: contacts with the neighbouring teeth, fit, bite and shade. Bonding
    or cementing of the piece.
  - >-
    Check of the bite, a control radiograph if needed, then upkeep at the
    regular follow-up.
faq:
  - question: How many appointments?
    answer: >-
      Once the mouth is stabilised, usually two or three: the preparation and
      impression, a try-in if needed, then the fitting.
  - question: 'Zirconia, ceramic or resin?'
    answer: >-
      It depends on the tooth, the force of your bite and the look you want.
      Zirconia is very strong. Pressed ceramic and layered porcelain give the
      most lifelike translucency. Printed resin is mostly used for temporary
      restorations. We propose what suits your case.
