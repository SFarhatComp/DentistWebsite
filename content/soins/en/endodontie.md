whenToConsult:
  - Spontaneous or nighttime dental pain
  - Prolonged sensitivity to hot or cold
  - Abscess or swelling
  - Fractured tooth with pulp exposure
treatmentSteps:
  - Clinical and radiographic diagnosis
  - Pulp vitality testing
  - Local anesthesia and isolation
  - Cleaning and shaping of the canals
  - Filling and temporary restoration
  - Final restoration (a crown is often recommended)
options:
  - Initial root canal treatment
  - Endodontic retreatment
  - Referral to an endodontist for complex cases
limits: Endodontic success depends on anatomy, patient cooperation, and the final restoration. Some cases (vertical fractures, complex anatomy) may require extraction.
aftercare: Sensitivity may persist for a few days. Avoid biting hard on the treated tooth until the final restoration is placed.
faq:
  - question: Is a root canal painful?
    answer: With effective anesthesia, the treatment is comfortable. The initial pain comes from the infection, which is resolved by the procedure.
  - question: Why is a crown recommended afterward?
    answer: A tooth that has undergone endodontic treatment is more fragile. A crown protects it against fractures.
  - question: How many visits are required?
    answer: One to two visits on average for the initial treatment.

Endodontics preserves a tooth whose pulp is infected or irreversibly inflamed. Rather than extracting it, we clean, disinfect, and seal the interior of the tooth.

The studio uses modern protocols — rotary instrumentation, irrigation, strict isolation — to maximize comfort and prognosis. The final restoration (often a crown) is planned from the outset.
